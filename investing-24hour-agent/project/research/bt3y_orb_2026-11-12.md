# ORB-15 earnings-gapper sleeve: 3-year backtest (research only)

Written 2026-11-12. RESEARCH ONLY: scripts/bakeoff.py, sleeves/, the book and the scheduled tasks were not touched. The bake-off rules stay frozen; this is informational.

## Verdict

**It would very likely fail the pre-registered bar, on all three counts.**

- **Trade count:** this is the hardest wall. Over 3 years it averaged **about 17 trades per 29 sessions** (verified-timing run), against a bar of 40. Only 25 of 724 rolling 29-session windows reached 40, all of them at the peak of earnings season. The bake-off window (Nov 12 to Dec 23) falls *after* earnings season. The same window in prior years had **7 trades (2023), 2 (2024) and 6 (2025)**.
- **Return:** negative after costs in every calendar year: -13.5% over 3 years at 5 bp/side and -22.4% at 10 bp/side.
- **Return per unit of exposure:** -2.26 at 5 bp, against SPY's +72.4% over the same span. No 29-session window passed all three tests in the main run.

Before costs the edge is roughly zero: an average of -0.008 R a trade and -3.5% over 3 years at 0 bp. The 0.1% round-trip cost on about 24% notional turns it clearly negative.

## Setup

- **Span:** 2023-11-13 to 2026-11-11, 752 sessions (the full 3 years; nothing was cut short).
- **Rules:** exactly as in sleeves/README.md section 3 and `bakeoff.py orb_day`.
  - `orb_trade()`, `addm()` and every ORB_* constant are AST-extracted from scripts/bakeoff.py at run time, not retyped.
  - The day loop mirrors `orb_day` line for line: gap on the first IEX 9:30-9:45 bar open, $10 and $20M ADV, the SPY 9:30-9:45 < -0.5% block, SIP 2x OR-volume (10 prior sessions, 15% fallback), 0.5-4% width, and the first 5-min close above the OR high from 9:45 to 11:30.
  - Sizing and limits: 0.5% risk, 25% notional cap, 100% total, max 4 trades a day (first by trigger time, ties to the bigger gap).
  - Exits: stop at the range midpoint, half off at +1R with the stop moved to breakeven, remainder out at the 15:30 open. Stops fill at min(open, stop), and the stop wins when a bar touches both levels.
  - Costs use the same formulas as `open_pos`/`close_pos`. Both legs count as one round trip. Exposure is computed as in `score_new`: weight × market minutes held ÷ market minutes in the span.
  - Equity compounds from $100k.
- **Reporters:** Nasdaq earnings calendar.
  - Sources: data/hist/earnings.csv through 2026-10-27, then research/earnings_runup/data/cal_new.csv.
  - Applied with the `orb_list` rule: after-hours or no time on the prior session, pre-market or no time today.
  - Membership is point-in-time S&P 500 from data/hist/sp500_hist_components.csv. After its last row (2026-08-18), that row is combined with the current screen.sp500() list.
  - Coverage looks complete: 260-600 S&P reporter-rows in every earnings month and about 50-70 in the off months. 11,519 reporter-days in total.
- **Report timing ("verified", the main run):**
  - The historical calendar has almost no report times. 96% of rows say "time-not-supplied", so the rule as coded counts each name on both its calendar day and the next session.
  - The live calendar usually carries times. To get closer to live, every one of the 519 triggered candidates was checked against its first Benzinga results headline (same matcher as research/earnings_runup/fetch.py).
  - Result: 451 timed correctly, 34 dropped because the release fell outside the prior 15:55 to today 09:35 window, and 34 with no headline, which were kept as live keeps time-less rows.
  - The "loose" run keeps all 519. Results barely differ.
- **Data:** Alpaca IEX 5-min bars on the trade day (as live), SIP 9:30-9:40 bars for the volume test (the fallback was never needed), and Alpaca SIP raw daily bars for the prior close and ADV. Live uses Nasdaq daily bars, which are near-identical.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Names added from shadow/gaps.csv** have no history, so they are not modelled. Live can trade a few extra names a day.
- **Report timing:** 34 triggered events (6.5%) had no Benzinga results headline and were kept, as the live rule does. The loose run brackets the effect, and the conclusion holds either way.
- **IEX bars set the OR high and low, the entries and the exits,** as live does. IEX is a thin feed, so prints can differ from consolidated prices, and the same applies live.
- **The SPY benchmark is price return** (unadjusted closes, as the score uses). Total return would be about 1.3 points a year higher.
- **Survivorship:** point-in-time membership is used. Delisted names have Alpaca bars when they traded, and no event lacked daily bars.
