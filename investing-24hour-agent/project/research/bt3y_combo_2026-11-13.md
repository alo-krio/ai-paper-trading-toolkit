# Combined sleeve backtest: Earnings Run-Up + Dip Buyer (fixed breaker), Nov 13 2023 to Nov 11 2026 (RESEARCH ONLY)

## Pre-registered bar (written 2026-11-13 08:55 ET, before any combo run; nothing tuned afterwards)
Primary run: combined $100k sleeve, 10 bp/side, rsi2 on the point-in-time S&P 500 universe, Run-Up on its backtest universe (S&P 500 PIT + today's Nasdaq-100) with the same filters.
Over the full 3 years (sessions 2023-11-13 to 2026-11-11, base = 2023-11-10 close), PASS requires all three:
- (a) total return / average exposure > SPY total return over the same span (SPY exposure = 1);
- (b) total return > 0;
- (c) max drawdown (session closes) smaller in magnitude than SPY's -18.8%.
Holdout: the last 6 months, sessions 2026-05-12 to 2026-11-11 (base = 2026-05-11 close), sliced from the same continuous run, same three checks against SPY over that holdout (SPY's own holdout max DD for (c)). Reported separately; the full-3-year line is the verdict, the holdout is a stated second read.
Exposure = capital-weighted market minutes held (entry weight x minutes), as in bakeoff.score_new and the rsi2 study.
Not used for the verdict: the 5 bp runs, the "today" rsi2 universe, any sensitivity, and the Russell-1000-like Run-Up variant.

---

## Verdict (primary: combo, 10 bp/side, rsi2 on point-in-time S&P 500)
**Full 3 years: PASS (narrowly). Holdout (May 12 to Nov 11 2026): FAIL on drawdown.**
- (a) Return per unit of exposure: 0.879 vs SPY 0.788. **Pass** by about 12%.
- (b) Total return: +65.7%. **Pass.**
- (c) Max drawdown: -14.1% vs SPY -18.8%. **Pass.**
- Holdout: (a) 0.150 vs SPY 0.033, pass. (b) +12.1%, pass. (c) **-14.1% vs SPY's -5.9% over the same 6 months: FAIL.** The whole-period max drawdown sits inside the holdout (peak Aug 13 2026, trough Nov 5 2026).
- The Dip Buyer is what costs the holdout. It contributed **-3.4%** of equity in the holdout and **-10.7% in 2026**, while Run-Up contributed +13.6% and +22.2%.
- **Run-Up alone** (same engine, 10 bp, same data) passes all three on both the full period and the holdout: +47.2%, return per exposure 1.69, max DD -10.8%; holdout +9.6%, max DD -5.7% vs SPY -5.9%. Adding the Dip Buyer raises total return by about 18 points but cuts return per exposure roughly in half and deepens the drawdown. That is an observation, not a rule change.

## How it was built (no rule changes to either sleeve)
- **Script:** `research/bt3y/combo/combo_bt.py`. It imports `research/bt3y/runup/runup_bt.py` (data, event table, filters' helpers) and `research/bt3y/rsi2_bt.py` (load/prep/RSI and all constants) unmodified.
- **Engine check:** run alone inside the combo engine, Run-Up reproduces the Nov 12 study (220 trades, +47.2% at 10 bp vs +47.3% reported; the 0.1-point gap is marking at liquidation value) and rsi2 "full minus breaker" reproduces exactly (1,037 trades, +16.8% at 10 bp, +38.9% at 5 bp).
- **Order inside each session (daily-bar proxies, as both studies):**
  1. Open (9:45 proxy): mark combined equity (feeds the breaker peak); rsi2 and Run-Up exits at the open.
  2. Close (15:30 proxy): rsi2 and Run-Up exits at the close (pre-report exits, hold-through halves, stops).
  3. Close (15:45 proxy): **Run-Up entries first**, min(20% of combined equity, cash), skipped if < 10% of equity; max 5 positions, 2 per sector. Then the breaker check. Then **rsi2 entries sized at 1/6 of combined equity from what cash is left** (skipped below half a slot), max 6.
- **No forced rsi2 sells.** When cash was short, the Run-Up candidate was skipped. That happened 114 times at 10 bp (median 2 rsi2 positions held at the time). Run-Up took 193 trades in the combo vs 220 alone. Run-Up keys a report as "seen" on first evaluation, as in its own backtest, so a skipped candidate is not retried the next day.
  - Clean alternative, noted but **not tested** (it would be a new rule): reserve cash for Run-Up candidates whose report is 5 sessions out and that already pass the filters the day before, or cap rsi2 at (equity minus 20% x open Run-Up slots). Both need pre-registration.
- **Fixed breaker:** 10% below peak of combined equity blocks **rsi2 entries** for 5 sessions; when the block ends, the peak resets to the current equity. Trips at 10 bp: 2026-04-27 and 2026-10-13. Run-Up entries are not blocked by it (Run-Up's own rules have no breaker). Sensitivity with the breaker blocking both: listed below.
- **Same name in both sleeves:** allowed (each sleeve keeps its own book). It never happened in these runs.
- **Exposure:** entry weight x market minutes held / (sessions x 390), as `bakeoff.score_new`. For Run-Up this reads 27.9% vs the 23% close-based figure in the Nov 12 report (different measure, same trades).
- **Costs:** per side on traded value; equity marked at liquidation value.

- The frozen breaker locked rsi2 out from 2026-10-13 to the end (it re-tripped every 5 sessions), which is why its drawdown is shallower: it stopped trading. The fix does what it was meant to (rsi2 resumes after the block).
- **Path sensitivity is high.** 5 bp beats 10 bp in total (+81.6% vs +65.7%) but has a deeper drawdown (-17.9% vs -14.1%), because the breaker and the cash queue change which trades get taken. A 4-point swing in max DD from a cost change means check (c) at -14.1% vs -18.8% is less safe than it looks.

## Extra variant: Run-Up alone on a wider Russell-1000-like universe (one shot, no tuning, not used for the verdict)
Script: `research/bt3y/combo/runup_wide.py` (swaps the data in `runup_bt.py`; rules unchanged). Universe each day: every stock in `research/bt3y/data/daily.parquet` (today's live universe + every S&P 500 member since 2023, ETFs removed) with prior close >= $10 and prior 20-day $ volume >= $50M. - More trades, as hoped, but the per-trade edge roughly halves and is no longer statistically clear (t 1.44). The extra names add count and almost no excess (+0.09%). Drawdown doubles.
- **Big caveat:** report timing is unknown for most names outside the base list (14,096 of 20,801 events used the "exit 2 sessions before the report" fallback; Benzinga/habit timing covers only the base names; the calendar's own AMC/BMO covered 167). Those trades miss the last 1-2 run-up sessions, which biases the wide edge **down**. The non-S&P names are today's live list, which biases it **up** (survivorship). Net direction unknown.

## Caveats
- Daily-bar proxies for the 15:45 / 15:30 / 9:45 prints, as in both source studies. Headline, supply, M&A and 8-K filters are not replicated for either sleeve (they only remove trades).
- Run-Up uses today's Nasdaq-100 list (no history; the S&P-only rerun on Nov 12 cut its 3-year return from +53.9% to +39.0%). The "today" rsi2 universe is survivorship-biased, yet it is the weaker result here (+4.0% combo at 10 bp), so the primary S&P PIT result is not the flattering one; but the 60-point gap between the two universes shows how fragile the Dip Buyer leg is.
- The holdout is not a true out-of-sample test of the rules (both sleeves' rules predate it, but the Nov 12 studies already looked at 2026). It is a held-aside read of the most recent regime.
- Sharpe uses rf 0. SPY is total return (adjusted closes).
